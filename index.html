2. Debunking longevity — What actually moves the needle
0:05 – 0:15 · 10 min · 2 slides
Attendees will leave this block able to answer
  • Which four things does the internet sell as “longevity” that are not worth doing?
  • What three inputs, done consistently, drive the biomarkers that matter?
  • Why did a $12-a-day routine outperform a $2M-a-year one?
The four myths you debunk, by name
  • Myth 1 — Exotic supplement stacks (NAD IVs, rapamycin, expensive peptides) as a shortcut.
  • Myth 2 — Wearable-obsession loops: chasing HRV, sleep-score gaming, glucose theater.
  • Myth 3 — Extreme protocols people cannot sustain past six weeks (36-hour fasts, cold plunges as therapy).
  • Myth 4 — Cardio-only or lifting-only training. Neither is enough on its own.
What actually works, in one sentence each
  • Sleep architecture is the recovery layer everything else runs on.
  • Whole-food nutrition at target protein is the biggest single lever most people ignore.
  • Mixed-modal training (strength, zone-2, sprint) is what shows up in VO2-max and strength markers.
Sample line “Bryan Johnson spends two million dollars a year. I spent about twelve dollars a day and ranked ahead of him. That is not a mystery. It is what happens when the boring things get done every day, and the expensive things get skipped.”

4. Pillar 2 · Food
0:25 – 0:35 · 10 min · 3 slides
Attendees will leave this block able to answer
  • How much protein do they need, in grams, at their bodyweight?
  • What is on the plate at every meal, and what is not?
  • When is the eating window open, and when is it closed?
The exact rules Julie follows
Vegetables 1 lb (450 g) per day
Protein 1 g per lb of goal bodyweight
Eating window 8:00 AM – 6:00 PM
Coffee cutoff 10:00 AM
Alcohol zero on training days
Processed food zero, seven days a week
The plate rule (this is the takeaway slide)
  • Half the plate: vegetables, one type cooked, one type raw.
  • A quarter of the plate: a single palm-sized portion of whole-food protein.
  • A quarter of the plate: a fist of a whole starch or a fruit.
  • One thumb of a whole fat: olive oil, avocado, nuts.
The trap
  • Using supplements to compensate for a plate that is wrong. Supplements are the last 5%, not the first 80%.
Sample line “If you eat this plate at every meal, in this window, drop the alcohol on training days, and stop the coffee at ten, you will move more biomarkers in ninety days than any pill on the market will move in a year.”
5. Pillar 3 · Movement
0:35 – 0:45 · 10 min · 3 slides
Attendees will leave this block able to answer
  • How many strength sessions a week, and what movements?
  • How much zone-2 cardio, at what heart rate?
  • What is the one interval session that raises VO2-max the fastest?
The weekly training pattern Julie uses
  • Strength × 3 — full body, compound lifts (squat, hinge, push, pull, carry). 45 minutes each.
  • Zone-2 × 2 — 45 minutes at conversational pace, heart rate roughly 60 – 70% of max.
  • Sprint interval × 1 — 4 × 4 minutes hard / 3 minutes easy on a bike or hill.
  • Steps daily — a floor of 8,000 steps every day, not a target on peak days only.
The two traps
  • Cardio-only routines that never build the muscle that keeps people independent past 70.
  • Lifting-only routines that neglect the VO2-max that predicts all-cause mortality.
One workout structure for Monday
  • 3 sets of goblet squats, 3 sets of push-ups, 3 sets of rows, a 20-minute walk after. That is the whole first session. Repeat Wednesday and Friday.
