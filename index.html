iCourse description

The Longevity Masterclass is a one-hour live class taught by Julie Gibson Clark, whose DunedinPACE of 0.665 placed her second on the Rejuvenation Olympics leaderboard on a daily health spend of approximately twelve dollars. The class translates her personal protocol into a repeatable operating system for anyone in the room: how she structures sleep, food, movement, and supplementation on an ordinary budget, and how those choices produced measurable biomarker outcomes. It is designed as the introductory session for students considering the eighteen-session Longevity Blueprint.

iiLearning outcomes

By the end of the hour, students will be able to:

  • Distinguish evidence-based longevity behaviors from popular myths and expensive dead ends.
  • State the sleep, nutrition, and movement rules Julie follows day to day, in numbers.
  • Reproduce a one-page daily schedule modelled on her routine.
  • Identify the entry-level supplement stack and its evidence basis.
  • Decide whether the Longevity Blueprint is the right next step for their goals.

iiiStructure

The class is delivered as one continuous sixty-minute session broken into six teaching blocks. There is no live Q&A. Every block follows the same LLA syllabus format: a short flow of teaching beats separated by arrows, followed by the takeaway students carry into their week.

Session OneThe full sixty minutes

Block 1 0:00 – 0:05 · 5 min

Framing the hour: what a twelve-dollar-a-day protocol actually produced

Welcome and course posture → Mini-lecture on DunedinPACE as a rate-of-aging biomarker and what a 0.665 score means → Julie's Rejuvenation Olympics ranking in context, leaderboard mechanics, biomarkers used, cost basis of competing protocols → Framing question: what is transferable from her routine to a working parent, and what is not → Preview of the four pillars that structure the rest of the hour.

Takeaway

Students leave with a clear definition of the outcome the class is teaching toward, and the honest limits of a one-hour intro.

Block 2 0:05 – 0:15 · 10 min

Debunking longevity: what does not move the biomarkers

Mini-lecture on the evidence hierarchy Julie uses to accept or reject a protocol → Four case walk-throughs: exotic supplement stacks (NAD IVs, rapamycin off-label, expensive peptides), wearable-score chasing, extreme protocols that fail past six weeks, single-modality training → Contrast frame against the two-million-a-year model, why consistency of the boring inputs outperformed it → Group synthesis: the three inputs that actually show up in the biomarkers.

Takeaway

Students can name four popular longevity behaviours that are not worth their money, and the three that are.

Block 3 0:15 – 0:30 · 15 min

Sleep and food: the two foundations, in exact numbers

Sleep segment (7 min) → Sleep architecture as the recovery layer everything else runs on → Julie's rules by number: lights out 9:00 PM, wake 5:00 AM, last meal three hours before bed, screens off sixty minutes before bed, bedroom cool and dark → The wearable-anxiety trap and how she avoids it → Food segment (8 min) → Plate architecture (protein floor, fibre lever, whole-food default) → Julie's rules by number: one pound of vegetables per day, one gram of protein per pound of goal bodyweight, eating window 8:00 AM to 6:00 PM, coffee cutoff 10:00 AM, zero alcohol on training days → The plate rule as the single change most students will make first.

Takeaway

Students leave with two changes they can make on Monday: move dinner to 6:00 PM, and rebuild one plate to the plate rule.

Block 4 0:30 – 0:40 · 10 min

Movement: the weekly pattern that shows up in VO2-max and strength

Mini-lecture on the two failure modes (cardio-only, strength-only) and why mixed-modal training is the pattern that predicts healthspan → Julie's weekly template: three strength sessions built around compound lifts, two zone-2 sessions at conversational pace, one sprint-interval session, and an eight-thousand-step daily floor → Live demonstration of a starter session students can run on Monday with no equipment → How progression works over the first twelve weeks → Why the walk after the biggest meal of the day earns its slot.

Takeaway

Students leave with a weekly training template and a first Monday session they can begin without a gym.

Block 5 0:40 – 0:55 · 15 min

The day, hour by hour, and the one-hundred-dollar supplement stack

Live walk-through of a full day, 5:00 AM to 9:00 PM: wake, water and light exposure, training window, meals, work, wind-down, sleep → Where the pillars intersect and where they compete for the same hour → Supplement segment → Evidence-based foundation set: creatine monohydrate 5 g daily, vitamin D3 5,000 IU with K2 in the morning with fat, combined EPA/DHA 2 g in the morning with food, magnesium glycinate 300 to 400 mg in the evening, whey or plant protein as needed to reach the daily target → Total monthly spend approximately one hundred dollars → Why the stack is the last five percent, not the first eighty.

Takeaway

Students leave with a copy of the daily schedule and the four-item supplement stack, priced and dosed.

Block 6 0:55 – 1:00 · 5 min

The next step: from the hour into the Blueprint

Recap of the four pillars in one sentence each → Framing of what the eighteen-session Blueprint adds: personal continuous glucose monitor lab, blood panel design, women-specific physiology, movement progression, faculty office hours, small-group cohort → Who the Blueprint is for and who it is not for → Warm invitation with a single next click. Class closes at exactly 60:00.

Takeaway

Students leave with a clear decision path on whether the Blueprint is their next step, and the one action to take in the next forty-eight hours.
