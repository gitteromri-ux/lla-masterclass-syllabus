First, at the bottom of the cake, sleep. I think of sleep as the night shift: the time your body rolls up its sleeves and gets to work on maintenance while we're busy dreaming. That's not just a poetic metaphor. Our brains have a built-in drainage system that flushes out the day's waste, and it mostly runs while we're asleep. Just a couple of years ago, researchers at Oregon Health and Science University managed to image these microscopic channels in living people for the first time. They call it the glymphatic system. Now, because I've suffered from insomnia before, I don't want anyone here getting into a middle-of-the-night shame spiral. I have been there. So let's focus on what we can control: consistent sleep timing and routine. A landmark 2023 study tracking over 60,000 adults found that sleep regularity predicts overall health and lifespan even better than sleep duration. The most regular sleepers saw up to a 48 percent drop in mortality risk. Same bedtime, every single day. Here are my rules. I run the same routine nearly every night starting right after dinner: last meal at least three hours before bed, a ten-minute post-dinner walk most days, screens off one hour before sleep, brush, floss, wash my face, and into bed. I use an eye mask and keep the room as cool as possible. My target window is 9 PM to 5 AM. I may not sleep through all eight hours, but I give myself that consistent opportunity. If you have kids, evening meetings, or a partner who thinks a 9 PM bedtime is a hostage situation, borrow the idea of an order and consistency, not my clock.
