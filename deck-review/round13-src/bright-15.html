I think of exercise the way I'd think of an investment portfolio. If everything you own is in one stock, you're not diversified, you're just optimistic. Most people do one thing very well and ignore the rest. Here are the four areas you should have in your movement portfolio. First, weights: resistance training, making your muscles work against a load. I build mine around compound lifts, movements that use several joints at once: squats, push-ups, lunges. Weightlifting is for your muscles, your bones, and increasingly scientists realize it's for your brain too. Second, VO2 max: how much oxygen your body can use when you're working hard, and it naturally decreases with age. You train it with intense cardio sprints followed by recovery. It's not what gets me out of bed, but it's the thing you need when you're sprinting through the airport to make your connection in Detroit so you can get to Italy as planned. It's for your heart and your lungs. Third, Zone 2: easy cardio at a conversational pace, walking or hiking. If you can chat with a friend while you do it, you're about right. It's for your metabolic health and your heart. And last, mobility and balance: your joints moving through their full range, through daily stretching or yoga, and staying steady on your feet. It's the one many forget, myself included. It's for your brain as much as your body, and it keeps you safe enough to keep doing the other three. The common failure is cardio-only or strength-only or yoga-only. If you're a runner, add the weights and the mobility. If you're a lifter, add some cardio and yoga. Here's what the data actually says. Grip strength: a major review pooled forty-eight studies across more than three million people; the stronger the grip, the lower the risk of dying, almost in a straight line. Steps: in July 2025 The Lancet Public Health pooled fifty-seven studies; seven thousand steps a day, compared to two thousand, came with a forty-seven percent lower risk of dying and a thirty-eight percent lower risk of dementia. Seven thousand, not ten. And balance: in late 2024 the Mayo Clinic tested people over fifty on grip, knee strength, walking and standing on one leg. The one-leg stand was the measure that fell fastest with age. Try it tonight. Shoes off, non-dominant leg, see how close you get to thirty seconds.
