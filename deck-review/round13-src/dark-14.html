Now on to the third layer: movement. I want to start with one picture of why it matters. Two people, the same age in calendar years. One stands strong and vibrant. One leans on a cane. Here's the study behind the picture. In December 2024, the Mayo Clinic measured the gap between how long people live and how long they live in good health, across 183 countries. Worldwide, the average is 9.6 years, about a decade of decline. In the United States it's 12.4 years, the widest gap in the world. Twelve and a half years, on average, at the end, in poor health. I'm in the middle of this right now with my parents: being unable to get to the store or the bathroom alone, losing the ability to handle emails and a phone, forgetting what's happening in the grandchildren's lives, falling in the middle of the night and needing the fire department. Not everything that happens towards the end is escapable. But in longevity science, exercise is widely recognized as the single most powerful intervention we have, impacting almost every hallmark of aging. The real question isn't how to live longer. It's whether we can shrink that window of decline down to almost nothing. The best defense is movement. But what movement?
