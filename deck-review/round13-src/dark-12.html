Sugar and glycemic stability. We just talked about what to add to our plates, but when it comes to longevity, what we subtract matters just as much: added sugar and simple carbs. Here's the problem in three words: spike, signal, scar. A spike is what you see on a glucose monitor after a sugary or simple-carb-rich meal. The signal is what that spike triggers inside you: a flood of insulin. Insulin is essential, intermittently. But when you eat foods that spike glucose throughout the day, that signal stays locked on high, and your cells function like a garage that keeps getting filled, never getting the clean-out it needs. That chronic over-signaling is recognized by longevity scientists as one of the hallmarks of aging. And then the scar. Glucose sticks to proteins in a process called glycation. Wrinkles are the visible proof: advanced glycation end-products, AGEs, are sugar cross-linking with the collagen and elastin in your skin, making them stiff and caramelized like the top of a crème brûlée. If that's happening on your face, it's happening inside: stiffening arteries, clouding the lenses of your eyes, wearing down the filters in your kidneys. That's why HbA1c is so powerful: it measures the percentage of sugar stuck to the protein in your blood, before permanent damage. In July 2024 a UCSF team measured the biological age of 342 women around forty, with the same kind of epigenetic clock behind my number, and every gram of added sugar came with an older biological age, even in women whose diets were otherwise healthy. They averaged sixty-one grams a day. A sweeping BMJ review points to a hard ceiling: keep added sugar under twenty-five grams a day, about six teaspoons. Now let's be real: no one, including me, is cutting sugar out for the rest of their life. Birthday cake is too precious a tradition. In its rightful place, once in a blue moon, it's okay. And a confession: I wore a glucose monitor for four weeks and braced for pizza to be the villain. Pizza barely moved it. The thing that spiked it was my healthy greens latte. You don't know until you look. That's why every student in the Academy wears one of these for two weeks.
